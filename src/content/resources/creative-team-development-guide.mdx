**WHO THIS IS FOR**

Creative directors and team leads who want their designers, writers, and content creators to actually get better under their leadership — not just stay busy.

**HOW TO USE IT**

Use this as a working reference through the year — the four practices don't need to happen all at once, but each one left out tends to show up later as stagnation or turnover.

## PRACTICE 1 — INDIVIDUAL GROWTH PLANS

- **Every creative on a team is at a different stage, working toward a different kind of growth. Treating the whole team the same way wastes the ambition of your strongest people and overwhelms your newest ones.**
- **Sit down with each team member twice a year to name where they want to grow — a skill, a specialty, a level of responsibility.**
- **Assign at least one project per quarter that stretches that specific area, not just whatever's next in the queue.**
- **Write the growth plan down. A verbal conversation gets forgotten by both sides within a month.**

## PRACTICE 2 — STRUCTURED CRITIQUE

- **Unstructured feedback — a quick "looks good" or a vague "something's off" — teaches people almost nothing. Structured critique is one of the fastest ways to develop a creative team's judgment, not just their output.**
- **Always separate what's not working from why it's not working, tied to the original objective.**
- **Ask the creator to explain their reasoning before offering your own — it reveals whether the gap is skill, or understanding of the brief.**
- **Give at least one specific thing that's working, not only what to fix — people repeat what gets named as good.**

## PRACTICE 3 — STRETCH ASSIGNMENTS

- **Skill grows fastest at the edge of someone's current ability, not comfortably inside it. Development stalls when every assignment matches exactly what someone already knows how to do.**
- **Deliberately assign work slightly above someone's current comfort level, with support available if they get stuck.**
- **Let junior team members present their own work to clients occasionally, with you there as a safety net.**
- **Rotate who leads a project, so leadership skill develops across the team, not only in one person.**

## PRACTICE 4 — VISIBLE PROGRESSION

- **Creative people leave teams — even ones they like — when they can't see where their growth leads. A visible path matters as much as the growth itself.**
- **Define what separates a junior, mid-level, and senior creative on your specific team, in writing.**
- **Make clear what it would take for someone to move from one level to the next.**
- **Celebrate progression publicly when it happens — a title change, a new responsibility, a client win credited by name.**

**THE REAL TEST**

If someone on your team left tomorrow for another opportunity, could you honestly say you'd done everything reasonable to help them grow here first? If not, that's the practice to start with.

## NEXT STEPS

**Developing people is slower and less visible than shipping projects — which is exactly why it's the practice most creative leads let slide under deadline pressure. Teams that keep it consistent end up with less turnover, better work, and creatives who actively want to stay.**
