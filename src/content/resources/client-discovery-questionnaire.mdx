**WHO THIS IS FOR**

Designers meeting a new client for the first time, or starting a new project with an existing one, who want structured answers instead of a vague verbal brief.

**HOW TO USE IT**

Send this ahead of a kickoff call so the client has time to think, then walk through the answers together — the follow-up conversation usually surfaces more than the written answers alone.

## 1. THE BUSINESS

- **In your own words, what does your business do, and for whom?**
- **How long has the business been running, and what's changed recently?**
- **Who are your 2–3 biggest competitors, and what do you think they do better than you?**

## 2. THE CUSTOMER

- **Describe your ideal customer as a real person — age, situation, what they care about.**
- **What do customers say when they explain why they chose you?**
- **What objection or hesitation comes up most before someone buys?**

## 3. THE PROJECT

- **What made you decide to start this project now?**
- **What does success look like when this is finished — be as concrete as possible.**
- **Is there a past design (yours or someone else's) that failed, and why do you think it did?**

## 4. TASTE & DIRECTION

- **Name 2–3 brands, in or outside your industry, whose look you admire — and say what specifically you like.**
- **Is there anything you definitely don't want — colors, styles, comparisons to avoid?**

## 5. PRACTICAL DETAILS

- **Decision-maker for final approval**
- **Hard deadline, if one exists, and why it's fixed**
- **Confirmed budget range**
- **Where the finished work will be used (print, web, signage, social, packaging)**

**IN THE FOLLOW-UP CALL**

Listen for gaps between what the client says and what they've actually built so far — a mismatch there is usually the real project, even if it's not what they asked for.

## NEXT STEPS

**Keep completed questionnaires on file for every client. Six months into a working relationship, this document is often the fastest way to settle a disagreement about what was actually agreed at the start.**
