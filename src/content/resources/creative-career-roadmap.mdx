**WHO THIS IS FOR**

Designers and creatives at any stage who want a clear sense of what comes next — and what actually needs to be true before moving to the next stage, rather than just more time passing.

**HOW TO USE IT**

Find the stage that matches where you are honestly today, not where you'd like to be. Each stage lists what defines it and what's usually required to move to the next one. Skipping stages tends to catch up with you later, usually around pricing or team management.

## THE FIVE STAGES

### Stage 1: The Learner

*Building foundational skill*

- **Comfortable in at least one professional design tool, not just tutorials.**
- **Has completed personal or practice projects, even unpaid ones.**
- **Understands basic design principles: hierarchy, spacing, type, color.**
- **Moves to Stage 2 when: someone outside their circle pays them for work, however small.**

### Stage 2: The Practitioner

*First paid work, portfolio building*

- **Has delivered paid projects for real clients, even at low rates.**
- **Building a portfolio of real (not spec) work.**
- **Still learning client communication, pricing, and scope — expect some painful lessons here.**
- **Moves to Stage 3 when: work is consistent enough to be a real income stream, not occasional gigs.**

### Stage 3: The Professional

*Consistent clients, pricing maturity*

- **Has a repeatable process: brief, proposal, contract, delivery.**
- **Prices based on value delivered, not just hours or guesswork.**
- **Has said no to at least one bad-fit client or underpriced project.**
- **Moves to Stage 4 when: demand consistently exceeds what they can personally deliver alone.**

### Stage 4: The Specialist or Lead

*Depth or delegation*

- **Either goes deep into a specific niche (packaging, UI, motion, branding) and commands premium rates for it, or begins leading other creatives on shared projects.**
- **Understands their own creative point of view well enough to say what they don't do.**
- **Starts managing other people's work quality, not only their own.**
- **Moves to Stage 5 when: they're ready to build something bigger than their own personal output.**

### Stage 5: The Principal

*Studio, agency, or recognized independent practice*

- **Runs a studio or a widely recognized independent practice with a clear identity in the market.**
- **Revenue no longer depends entirely on their own hours at the desk.**
- **Actively developing other creatives, not only producing work themselves.**
- **Is a known name clients or employers seek out, not one they stumble onto.**

## NEXT STEPS

**Most creative careers don't move through these stages in a straight line — expect to sit at one stage longer than expected, or briefly slide back after a hard year. What matters is knowing honestly which stage you're actually in, so you're building the right skill for where you are, not the one that looks impressive on a bio.**
