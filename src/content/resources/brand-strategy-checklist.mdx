**WHO THIS IS FOR**

Business owners and marketing leads about to brief a designer, launch a new offer, or go into a rebrand — who want the strategic groundwork solid before anyone opens a design file.

**HOW TO USE IT**

Work through each section as a gate. Don't move to the next section — and don't move to visual identity work — until every box in the current one is checked. Use it alone first, then again with your team.

## 1. FOUNDATIONS

If you can't check these five, nothing built on top of your strategy will hold.

- ☐ We can name our target customer specifically — not just a broad demographic like "young professionals."
- ☐ We have a one-sentence description of what business we're actually in.
- ☐ We know who else our customer could choose instead of us. _Direct competitors, indirect substitutes, and "doing nothing" all count._
- ☐ We can state our single biggest point of difference in one sentence, without hedging.
- ☐ We have at least one piece of real evidence that backs that difference up.

## 2. MARKET REALITY

Strategy built on assumptions instead of evidence tends to fail expensively, after the identity is already built.

- ☐ We've talked to real customers about why they chose us, or didn't. _Not guessed. Actual conversations, even five of them._
- ☐ We know our pricing relative to the two or three alternatives our customers actually compare us against.
- ☐ We understand what's changing in our market that could affect this positioning over the next 12 months.

## 3. INTERNAL ALIGNMENT

A strategy that only lives in the founder's head isn't a strategy the business can execute.

- ☐ Everyone on the team could repeat our positioning in their own words, not recite it word for word.
- ☐ Our current pricing, offer, and messaging don't quietly contradict the strategy we just wrote down.
- ☐ Leadership has signed off on the strategy in writing — not just nodded in a meeting.

## 4. READY FOR DESIGN & EXECUTION

This is the gate before you brief anyone on visuals.

- ☐ We have a documented brand strategy — a real document, not ideas scattered across someone's head and old chat threads.
- ☐ We know what's off-brand for us — what we will say no to, not only what we stand for.
- ☐ We have budget and timeline agreed before briefing a designer, so the strategy doesn't get compromised by a rushed execution.

**SCORING**

Checked fewer than 8 of 13 boxes? Go back to Sections 1 and 2 before spending on identity or campaign work. A strategy with real gaps gets far more expensive to fix after the logo, website, or packaging is already designed around it.

## NEXT STEPS

**A brand strategy is a working document, not a plaque on the wall — revisit it every time the market, the offer, or the competitive set shifts meaningfully. Once every box here is checked, you're ready to brief a designer with the Strategic Design Brief, or move straight into the Brand Identity Checklist.**
